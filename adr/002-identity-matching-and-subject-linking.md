# ADR – PID Attributes for Cross-Border Identity Matching

**Authors:**

- Malin Norlander, Bolagsverket, SE

## Context

Cross-border services need a reliable way to recognise the same identity in subsequent interactions.

This ADR focuses on new user registrations, where we can define which identifiers are stored. Matching against already registered users is out of scope, as this is limited by the attributes already available in existing systems.

The objective is to match the same identity, not the same person. A person with multiple nationalities may have independently issued PIDs representing different identities. Linking such identities is out of scope.

## Decision

### Within WE BUILD:

- The existing PID id attribute SHALL be mandatory.

- A nonce attribute SHALL be added and SHALL be mandatory.

- Both id and nonce SHALL be generated by the PID Provider.

- The nonce SHALL enable the wallet to generate a stable pseudonym unique to the identity and relying party.

The id provides deterministic matching where persistent identifiers can be used.

The nonce-based approach allows WeBuild to explore a privacy-preserving alternative where the relying party receives a stable identifier without receiving a globally reusable persistent identifier.

## Alternatives considered

### Persistent unique identifier

A persistent unique identifier is the only considered mechanism that provides deterministic matching rather than a probability that two identities are the same.

Some Member States do not allow persistent identifiers for their citizens to be exposed cross-border due to privacy concerns. We nevertheless make id mandatory in WeBuild to support existing requirements while exploring alternatives.

### Relying-party-specific pseudonym

A nonce generated by the PID Provider allows the wallet to derive a stable pseudonym for each relying party.

The same identity can therefore be recognised by the same relying party over time, while different relying parties receive different pseudonyms and cannot correlate the identity based on the identifier.

### Additional PID attributes

Using combinations of attributes such as name, date of birth or place of birth was rejected.

Additional attributes can increase the probability of a correct match but cannot provide deterministic matching. Non-static attributes may also change, making the match non-persistent, while additional attributes increase unnecessary disclosure of personal data.

## Consequences

The pseudonym mechanism must remain stable across PID renewal, wallet replacement and recovery to provide persistent matching.

The exact pseudonym derivation mechanism and lifecycle are defined separately.
