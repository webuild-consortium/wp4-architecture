## 1. Context and Problem Statement

The "Complementary Hybrid Model" (ADR-371) and the "Attestation Chaining Model" (ADR-168) establish that EAA Providers can issue legally compliant, decentralized, and offline-verifiable EAAs by chaining their European Business Wallet Owner Identity (EBWOID) directly in the EAA header.

However, for a Relying Party (verifier) to cryptographically validate this chain, there must be an unbroken link of trust between the private key used by the EAA Provider to sign the EAA and their legal identity (EUID).

In current legislative drafts, the EBWOID is defined primarily as a static identity attestation. While the draft Business Wallet Regulation [COM(2025) 838] requires the EBWOID to be cryptographically bound to the wallet unit (typically represented by the standard `cnf` public key), this device-binding key pair is intended only for interactive session authentication and mutual identification. It should **not** be used to protect the origin and integrity of EBW-issued EAAs.

Therefore, the EBWOID lacks a dedicated, standardized attribute to assert the owner's public signing key used specifically for EAA issuance. Without this dedicated cryptographic binding, verifiers cannot authenticate the EAA's signature offline, and the attestation chaining model remains unviable.

### The Connection to Mutual Identification (ADR-205)
This cryptographic gap also directly impacts wallet-to-wallet mutual identification. ADR-205 defines a **"Business Wallet-Native Model (Certificate-Free)"** for mutual identification in backend-to-backend (M2M) environments. Under this model, an EBW acting as a Verifier authenticates itself by signing its Presentation Request Object using the private key corresponding to the `signing_key` attested within its own EBWOID.

To make this certificate-free authentication path possible, the EBWOID must officially support and certify this dedicated signing key.

## 2. The Architectural Decision: EBWOID Cryptographic Extensions

To bridge this cryptographic trust gap and enable both decentralized EAA verification and certificate-free mutual identification, we agree to the following mandatory extensions and obligations for the EBWOID and its issuing EBWOID Providers:

1.  **Mandatory EAA Signing Key Attribute (`cnf_signing`):**
    The EBWOID credential **MUST** include a dedicated public key attribute, `cnf_signing`, representing the public key generated by the owner's wallet.
    *   This public key **SHALL** be embedded within the EBWOID payload as a dedicated `cnf_signing` claim.
    *   By signing the EBWOID containing this attribute, the EBWOID Provider cryptographically confirms and certifies the binding of this signing key to the owner's legal identity (EUID).
    *   **Dual-Use Purpose:** This key pair is used both by EAA Providers to sign issued EAAs (ADR-168) and by EBWs acting as Verifiers to sign Request Objects in the wallet-native mutual identification flow (ADR-205).
    *   This maintains strict cryptographic separation of duties from the interactive device-binding key (`cnf`), which remains reserved for session-level authentication.

2.  **EBWOID Provider Header Obligation:**
    To allow verifiers to establish trust in the EBWOID offline, the header of the EBWOID **MUST** include the full certificate chain of the issuing EBWOID Provider. This chain must include:
    *   The **Root Certificate** of the provider.
    *   The **Sealing Trust Service Certificate** (acting as an intermediate certificate listed in the Trusted List / TLoL).
    *   The **Seal Certificate** used by the EBWOID Provider to sign the EBWOID.

3.  **Automatic Chain Inheritance:**
    When the EAA Provider signs an EAA, they embed their EBWOID in the EAA's header. Because the EBWOID header contains the EBWOID Provider's certificate chain, the final EAA automatically inherits the entire cryptographic chain up to the Trusted List root, enabling full offline validation by the verifier.

## 3. Consequences of the Decision

### 3.1 What becomes easier?

*   **Enables Certificate-Free Mutual Identification (ADR-205):** By officially attesting the `cnf_signing` key within the EBWOID, EBWs can fully execute the "Business Wallet-Native Model" defined in ADR-205. Requesters can sign Request Objects using this key, **completely eliminating the need for external X.509 Access Certificates and registry registrations** for EBW-to-EBW traffic.
*   **Eliminates Administrative TLoL Bottlenecks:** EU Member States do not need to register or maintain millions of businesses as individual EAA providers on national Trusted Lists. The administrative overhead for Member States is reduced to zero.
*   **Worldwide and Decoupled Verifiability:** The identity of the EAA Provider is verifiable offline worldwide by any entity that has cached the free-of-charge Trusted List. Trust is maintained even if the EAA Provider is offline or ceases to exist, supporting long-term compliance use cases.
*   **Provider Autonomy:** EAA Providers can issue attestations autonomously using their own wallets once they possess a valid EBWOID.
*   **Simplified Delegation of Authority:** The hierarchical model provides a natural and secure pattern for delegating authority. It becomes extremely easy to issue delegation attestations (e.g., Power of Representation, Power of Attorney, or Mandates) from legal entities to other natural or legal persons, without requiring central registry updates.
*   **Extensibility to IoT and Autonomous Agents:** The model seamlessly extends to object wallets, machines, and AI agents containing wallet functionality. A parent enterprise can issue identity keys directly to its devices (e.g., truck wallets), enabling secure machine-to-machine transactions without the administrative nightmare of registering millions of individual devices in national registries.

### 3.2 What becomes more difficult?

*   **EBWOID Schema & Rulebook Updates:** The official We Build EBWOID rulebook and its underlying JSON schemas must be modified to include the new `cnf_signing` attribute and the mandatory header structure.
*   **EBWOID Provider Alignment:** EBWOID Providers must be willing and technically equipped to accept the EAA signing public key generated by the owner's wallet during the EBWOID issuance process and include it in the issued credential.

### 3.3 How do we address the risks introduced by this change?

To mitigate the risk of **EBWOID Provider Alignment** and the technical complexity of injecting the `cnf_signing` key during issuance:

*   **Leverage Existing Standards:** The mechanism required to inject and include the `cnf_signing` key during EBWOID issuance is functionally identical to the standard mechanism used today to include the interactive `cnf` device-binding key.
*   **Standard Protocol Flow:** During the standard OpenID4VCI issuance process, the wallet generates the EAA signing key pair and proves possession of the private key via a standard Proof of Possession (PoP) challenge. The resulting public key is passed to the EBWOID Provider API.
*   Since EBWOID Providers and wallet vendors **must already implement this protocol flow** to support standard `cnf` device binding, expanding the implementation to support an additional, parallel `cnf_signing` parameter represents a minimal, low-risk technical delta.

## 4. Advice

Once merged, this is our consortium’s decision. This decision represents a critical technical compromise that enables We Build's primary decentralized use cases to function securely, legally, and scalably within the boundaries of the existing eIDAS v2 framework.
